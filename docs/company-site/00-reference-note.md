# Reference note: structure and copy patterns we take

**Access caveat.** This environment's network policy blocked live fetches of mechanical-orchard.com, distyl.ai, ycombinator.com, lab0.ai and ontora.ai. The notes below come from search-engine excerpts of those pages (titles, page summaries, launch-post text) and not from reading the live pages. Before launch, someone should open the live pages and check this note against them, especially Mechanical Orchard's about-us team cards, the PoC form fields and the legal page headings.

1. Mechanical Orchard, platform page: Imogen is described as a small set of named modules (Analyze first), each with one job and one output. Our [engine name] page uses 4 named stages, one sentence of input and one of output each.
2. Mechanical Orchard, insights ("Verify, then trust"): the core idea is a closed loop. Record what the old system does, generate new code, prove it equivalent, repeat. Our thesis and our Verify stage are built on this argument, in our own words and with our own four measures.
3. Mechanical Orchard, PoC ("See Imogen work on your code"): free, NDA-covered, scoped to one first replica plus a roadmap. Our "See it work on your system" page keeps that scope: one workflow, a verification report, and a written price.
4. Mechanical Orchard, about-us and footer: a mission line, then plain team cards (photo, name, title, short line). We use one card. Legal pages are separate, plainly titled documents linked from the footer.
5. Distyl: people who own the outcome plus an internal platform (Distillery) that captures what each engagement teaches. We take the "the system improves with each engagement" idea. Unlike Distyl, we never sell the platform.
6. Distyl careers: a trait list built on contrasts, interviews on real problems instead of puzzles, AI use encouraged and judged on how the candidate checks AI output. Our Careers page uses the same structure.
7. Lab0 and Ontora: the headline compresses time against the incumbent ("six months" against "ten days"; "months" against "24 hours"). We state 21 days against a bracketed incumbent figure, with the source named.
8. Foaster: one plain paragraph on who does what (agents do the work, human experts review). Our human/agent block does the same, and adds who signs off.
9. Perfectly: payment tied to the outcome (paid only when a role is filled) and results as multipliers (4×, 10×). We tie the balance to acceptance and state results as ratios against a named comparison.
10. What we leave out: metaphor headlines (Ontora's "read your company like a book"), "AI-native [incumbent]" taglines (Foaster), logo walls, and lists of industries served (Distyl). These either break our copy rules or claim things we cannot show.
